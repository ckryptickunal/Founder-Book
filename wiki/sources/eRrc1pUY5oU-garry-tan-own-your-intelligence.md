# Garry Tan: Own Your Intelligence

## Summary

Garry Tan argues that individuals should move beyond 'corporate AGI'—rented, limited chatbots—and instead build 'personal AGI' by creating a private, owned infrastructure of their own life data (a 'library') and agent-driven workflows ('skill files'). By treating their own knowledge and history as a proprietary asset, individuals can achieve massive leverage, effectively becoming a '100x' worker or founder without needing traditional permission or large teams.

## Key Ideas

- Personal AGI is an agent that runs on your own infrastructure, reads your own data, and compounds in value over time.
- The 'library' (your personal history, notes, and emails) combined with a 'librarian' (AI agent) is the core of personal intelligence.
- Skill files are simple, plain-English instructions that allow AI agents to execute complex, recurring tasks.
- Intelligence should be owned, not rented; relying on corporate AI platforms risks 'lobotomizing' your workflows if they pivot or change terms.
- The 'conatus'—the drive to increase one's power to act—is the engine of productivity, and AI agents amplify this by removing the limits of human working memory.
- Building in public and owning your own cognitive 'skill files' is a form of professional sovereignty.

## Notable Claims

- AI agents can increase individual productivity by 8x to 400x. Evidence: Garry Tan's personal comparison of his coding output in 2013 versus his current output at YC.
- The fastest-growing startups are those that treat AI as a workforce rather than just autocomplete. Evidence: Observations of YC Winter 25 batch companies with 95% AI-generated codebases.
- Markdown is effectively code for modern AI models. Evidence: The ability of LLMs to execute complex logic when provided with clear, structured English instructions in markdown files.
