# Gmail creator Paul Buchheit on the very first version of Google’s “Did you mean?” feature

## Metadata

- Video ID: `_ZR1dWrkogA`

## Summary

Paul Buchheit, the creator of Gmail, recounts the origin of Google's "Did you mean?" spell correction feature. He explains that his personal struggle with spelling, combined with observing that approximately a third of Google's search queries were misspelled, motivated him to implement an early version of the spell corrector. He built the initial feature using an existing library, noting its early limitations such as incorrect suggestions for terms like "Turbo Tax."

## Key Ideas

- The "Did you mean?" feature was one of Google's earliest and most impactful additions.
- The feature's creation was personally motivated by Paul Buchheit's difficulty with spelling.
- Buchheit identified a significant problem in Google's query logs, with about one-third of queries containing misspellings.
- He developed the first "Did you mean" feature using an existing spell corrector library.
- Early versions of the spell corrector had limitations, sometimes producing nonsensical corrections.

## Notable Claims

- Approximately a third of Google's search queries were misspelled. Evidence: Paul Buchheit states, 'like a third of the queries were misspelled or something like that.'
- Implementing the spell corrector was 'the easiest quality win ever' for Google. Evidence: Buchheit remarks, 'so it was like the easiest quality win ever was just to fix the spelling.'
- The initial spell corrector could produce 'dumb Corrections.' Evidence: Buchheit provides the example: 'if you typed in Turbo Tax it would try to correct it to turbot axe turbot being a type of fish.'
