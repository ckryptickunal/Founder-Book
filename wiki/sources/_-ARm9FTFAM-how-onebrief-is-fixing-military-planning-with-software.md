# How Onebrief Is Fixing Military Planning With Software

## Metadata

## Summary

Onebrief, co-founded by former US Army officer Grant Demory, develops software to dramatically accelerate military operational planning, a process traditionally reliant on Word and PowerPoint and often taking years for major plans. The company has achieved significant growth (57x year-over-year, 144 people, $650M valuation Series C). Demory shares insights into selling to the complex US military, emphasizing the strategy of targeting individual commands with smaller budgets (government purchase cards up to $25k) to gain initial access and user exposure. He details early challenges like lack of user access and Authority to Operate (ATO), which they circumvented through 'hacks' like Saturday exercises with paid planners and deploying software on a single, isolated laptop in a secure facility. A pivotal moment was securing a $322k contract with US Pacific Fleet, which sponsored Onebrief onto the military's secure networks (JWX), enabling broader deployment and collaboration. Demory highlights the ongoing 'three barriers' in defense tech (facilities clearance, ATO, contracting) and Onebrief's future vision to be the standard for military staff operations, making them faster, smarter, and more efficient, leveraging AI for mundane tasks and eventually automated course of action generation. He believes Onebrief's work is the 'most important thing happening in defense tech' due to its cost-effectiveness and impact on decision-making.

## Key Ideas

- Military planning is slow, inefficient, and relies on outdated tools (Word, PowerPoint), creating a significant bottleneck for military decisions.
- Onebrief's software dramatically speeds up military operational planning, reducing multi-year processes to hours and significantly improving military effectiveness.
- Selling to the US military, a complex customer, can be achieved by initially targeting individual commands with smaller budgets and leveraging 'hacks' like government purchase cards.
- Overcoming critical barriers like Authority to Operate (ATO) and gaining access to secure military networks (JWX) is essential for scaling defense software, often requiring creative, non-traditional approaches.
- AI has significant potential in military planning, from automating mundane tasks to generating advanced, superhumanly smart courses of action.
- When selling AI to the military, focus on tangible business outcomes and established trust rather than just the technology itself.
- Identifying and addressing the true bottlenecks early, even if they seem non-obvious, is crucial for success in defense tech entrepreneurship.
- Software solutions in defense tech offer high leverage and cost-effectiveness, enabling widespread impact on decision-making at a fraction of the cost of hardware.
