# Building the Agentic AI Platform for Hospitals

## Summary

Bunker Hill Health, led by Mish Khandwala, announced a $55 million Series B funding round to build an Agentic AI platform for health systems. The company aims to drastically lower the cost and increase the speed of iteration in healthcare, addressing the current challenges where AI's potential is clear but its adoption is hindered by high onboarding efforts and organizational friction. Their 'Care Bricks' platform provides a unified solution with three pillars: Knowledge (integrating diverse data sources), Reasoning (applying AI models like LLMs), and Action (automating patient notifications, provider messaging, and administrative tasks). This platform enables hospitals to deploy numerous AI-driven use cases rapidly, moving beyond single-point solutions and transforming healthcare from a laggard to an early adopter of AI, ultimately leading to a more connected and proactive patient experience.

## Key Ideas

- Healthcare suffers from high costs and slow speed of iteration, making AI adoption difficult despite its clear potential.
- The primary barrier to AI adoption in hospitals is not economic cost but the immense effort and time required for onboarding new tools due to procurement, IT, data privacy, and change management hurdles.
- Many AI solutions in healthcare share a similar underlying structure: extracting data from systems of record and processing it with large language models (LLMs).
- Bunker Hill Health's 'Agentic AI platform' (Care Bricks) provides a foundational solution with 'Knowledge,' 'Reasoning,' and 'Action' pillars to streamline AI deployment across diverse clinical and administrative use cases.
- LLMs have been a game-changer, simplifying algorithm development (many algorithms became prompts) and shifting focus to solving fundamental, 'bottom-of-the-pyramid' problems in healthcare operations.
- Effective AI deployment in healthcare relies heavily on 'tool use' (allowing LLMs to browse and search external information) and robust citation mechanisms to reduce hallucinations and build trust.
- AI performance in healthcare should be measured against the 'standard of care,' which often means comparing against 'doing nothing' due to human resource scarcity.
- Bunker Hill Health maintains a lean team by focusing engineers solely on platform primitives, allowing forward-deployed teams to configure solutions for diverse hospital workflows without custom coding.
- The platform enables a more connected and proactive patient journey, from incidental findings and early diagnosis to treatment prioritization, insurance processing, and automated follow-ups.

## Notable Claims

- Healthcare has a reputation for being slow to change and difficult to move the needle in. Evidence: Mish Khandwala states, 'healthcare has this reputation of it's hard to move the needle in or it takes a long time to get something done.'
- The effort required to onboard a new AI tool in healthcare far outweighs its specific benefits, often taking two years for a single function. Evidence: Mish Khandwala explains, 'the effort that it takes to onboard a new tool far outweighs the benefits. It's like you will spend 2 years trying to onboard a new tool and only for it to deliver one specific thing.'
- The core challenge for AI adoption in health systems is not the economic cost of tools, but the extensive work involved in deployment. Evidence: Mish Khandwala states, 'It's not so much the economic cost. That's there, but it's not the major factor for lack of adoption. It's more so it's just going to be so much work to do all of this.'
- Underneath the hood, many different AI solutions in healthcare are similar, involving data extraction from systems of record and processing with large language models. Evidence: Mish Khandwala notes, 'if you look underneath the hood of all of these sort of different solutions, they look very similar where they are extracting data from different systems of record... And then putting that through large language models.'
- The FDA approved Wegovy (Ozempic's cousin) for fatty liver disease (MASH) in August, creating a new opportunity for patient identification via AI. Evidence: Mish Khandwala mentions, 'last August, Wegovy, Ozempic's cousin got FDA approved for fatty liver disease for mash.'
- The advent of LLMs transformed many algorithms into simple prompts, making AI development much faster and more accessible. Evidence: Mish Khandwala states, 'Because now many algorithms just became prompts.'
- Tool use, rather than just inherent model improvement, has been the most impactful factor in reducing hallucinations and improving LLM reliability. Evidence: Mish Khandwala asserts, 'Tool use... has been like it has moved the needle a lot more in terms of reducing hallucinations than the just inherent the base model getting better and better.'
- AI performance in healthcare should always be compared against the 'standard of care,' not necessarily 100% accuracy. Evidence: Mish Khandwala states, 'I have been a strong believer that the performance comparison should not be 100%. It should always be against the standard of care.'
- Bunker Hill Health's engineering team exclusively works on the platform level (primitives), not on specific use cases. Evidence: Mish Khandwala explains, 'our engineering team only works on the the platform level. No one has ever written... no engineer is working on a specific use case.'
- Bunker Hill Health currently has more customers than the number of people on its team, demonstrating high efficiency. Evidence: Mish Khandwala states, 'we are 21 people today, and we have more customers than the number of people actually at this size.'
