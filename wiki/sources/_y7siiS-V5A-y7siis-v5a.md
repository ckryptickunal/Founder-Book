## Summary

Matt and Eric from Trigger.dev discuss their journey through Y Combinator, the evolution of their product, and the future of software with AI agents. Initially launching as an 'async background jobs framework' or 'Zapier for developers,' they iterated through different versions, eventually finding strong product-market fit by pivoting to a platform for executing AI agents on their infrastructure. They emphasize the importance of design, developer experience, and the strategic advantages of being open source. The founders highlight how the rise of AI has dramatically increased engineer productivity, influenced their hiring strategies (prioritizing candidates who leverage AI tools), and changed customer support dynamics. They also share their vision for the future of computing, focusing on programmatic checkpoint restores, and offer advice to new founders on shipping early and staying close to customers.

## Key Ideas

- Trigger.dev evolved from an 'async background jobs framework' to an 'AI agent execution platform' on their infrastructure, achieving product-market fit with the rise of AI.
- Strong emphasis on design and developer experience (DX), including a 'code-first' approach on their website and designing SDKs to prevent developer failure.
- Open-source (Apache 2) model provides a significant advantage, especially for LLMs as users, by increasing internet footprint and allowing AI to interact directly with their codebase.
- AI tools like Claude Code and Opus 4.5 have dramatically increased engineer productivity (5x-10x), leading to less aggressive hiring and a shift in hiring criteria to assess candidates' AI tool proficiency.
- The future of computing, according to Trigger.dev, involves programmatic snapshot and restore mechanisms (checkpoint restores) for freezing and resuming entire machine states.
- AI agents are leveraged beyond just code generation for tasks like benchmarking, analysis, and test generation, improving overall software quality and development efficiency.
- Key startup advice includes shipping early, continuously learning from customer feedback, staying close to users, and knowing when to persevere through product iterations despite a lack of immediate product-market fit.
