## Summary

Heart Aerospace, founded by Anders Forslund, is developing the ES-30, a hybrid-electric regional airliner designed to reduce the cost and environmental impact of short-haul air travel. By utilizing electric motors with minimal moving parts and a hybrid system for range, the company aims to modernize regional aviation infrastructure and compete with aging 40-year-old aircraft designs.

## Key Ideas

- Electric motors offer significantly lower maintenance and operating costs due to fewer moving parts compared to traditional jet engines.
- The ES-30 uses a hybrid-electric system to solve the battery reserve requirements and range limitations of pure electric flight.
- Regional air travel is currently inefficient due to the high cost and complexity of jet engines, which are optimized for larger, longer-haul aircraft.
- Hardware startups should prioritize building physical, touchable prototypes to secure capital and demonstrate progress.
- The company adopts a 'software-defined' approach to aviation, focusing on iterative testing and minimizing the impact of failure rather than just the probability of failure.
- Heart Aerospace is targeting the regional market rather than flying taxis, leveraging existing airport infrastructure.

- [[topics/regional-air-mobility|Regional Air Mobility]]: Focusing on short-haul flights under 2 hours to improve connectivity and reduce costs for underserved regional routes.
- [[topics/hardware-startup-strategy|Hardware Startup Strategy]]: The philosophy of building in-house, iterating rapidly, and using physical milestones to drive capital acquisition.

## Notable Claims

- Electric motors have zero wear compared to jet engines. Evidence: Electric motors have essentially one moving part and no combustion or fluids, unlike jet engines which have thousands of parts and operate at extreme temperatures.
- The ES-30 offers better operating economics than current regional planes. Evidence: The company claims a 48% improvement in operating economics, driven by lower fuel/electricity costs and reduced maintenance.
- The ES-30 is a 'software-defined' vehicle. Evidence: The design philosophy prioritizes software control and iterative testing to manage risk, similar to modern electric car architectures.
