# Hard Tech Startups Are Built One Milestone at a Time

## Metadata

## Summary

The founders of Heart Aerospace discuss the incremental development strategy for hard tech startups, emphasizing the importance of hitting tangible milestones—such as physical prototypes—to secure capital and customer interest.

## Key Ideas

- Hard tech startups must demonstrate material progress to secure subsequent rounds of funding.
- Physical prototypes are more effective at attracting investors and customers than abstract concepts.
- Conventional design aesthetics can be a strategic advantage over futuristic designs when trying to replace existing industry standards.
- Inbound leads from unexpected sources, including spam folders, can lead to major enterprise partnerships.

## Notable Claims

- Founders should ensure every capital raise is accompanied by a material, physical milestone. Evidence: The company moved from LOIs to a 400kW motor prototype, which then secured pre-orders from United Airlines.
- Conventional design is superior to futuristic design for electric aircraft adoption. Evidence: Heart Aerospace intentionally designed their plane to look like existing turboprops to avoid appearing like a 'sports car' and to emphasize reliability.

## Quotes

> Every time you need more capital, there needs to be something material. Ideally, something physical you can touch.
> I'd rather build something that looks very conventional, but is kind of hiding its Superman cape under the hood.
