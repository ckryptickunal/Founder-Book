# Zynga Founder: Consumer Is Not Investible Right Now - Thats Why You Should Build It

## Summary

Mark Pincus, founder of Zynga, discusses his product development philosophy and its application to the current AI landscape in an interview with Y Combinator. He introduces his 'Proven Better New' framework for innovation, drawing lessons from his experience across the web, social, and AI eras. Pincus highlights the critical role of 'trust' in building successful platforms and critiques the slow adoption and 'skill issue' in enterprise AI. Despite current investor sentiment deeming consumer products 'not investable,' he argues that the opportunity for AI-powered 'internet treasures' is unprecedented, predicting a consumer AI revolution around 2029 as compute costs plummet. He also emphasizes 'Founder Mode,' advocating for founders to trust their instincts, maintain presence, and foster a culture of intellectual honesty and resilience.

## Key Ideas

- A holistic, 'full-stack' approach to product development and company building is essential for long-term success.
- The 'Proven Better New' framework provides a structured method for innovation, distinguishing between copying proven successes, making incremental improvements, and testing novel, often incorrect, ideas.
- The tech investment landscape cycles between favoring consumer and enterprise, with investors often being '180 degrees off' from future opportunities.
- Trust is a foundational element for successful social networks and consumer platforms, as demonstrated by Facebook's early success compared to others.
- Current enterprise AI adoption faces challenges, including a 'skill issue' and inefficient use of tokens, while frontier models remain expensive.
- Despite current market sentiment, the opportunity for AI-powered consumer products ('internet treasures') is immense, with a predicted 'consumer revolution' around 2029 driven by decreasing AI compute costs.
- 'Founder Mode' emphasizes a founder's presence, deep product knowledge, intuition, and the courage to pivot, fostering a culture of intellectual honesty and resilience within the team.
- The 'Jevons Paradox' suggests that as AI compute becomes 'free' and unlimited, it will enable entirely new categories of consumer services and experiences.
- The 'free' business model, as exemplified by Freeloader and Zynga, is a powerful force that will reshape services in the age of AI.

## Notable Claims

- Even though consumer is arguably not investable right now, the opportunity has never been greater to offer people a new, internet treasure, reinvent some service that we thought was over or just generic, but it's enabled now because of AI and agents. Evidence: Pincus's opening statement and recurring theme, supported by his belief in AI's transformative power and the analogy to past tech cycles.
- I traced the beginning of social networking to Napster. Evidence: Pincus's personal historical perspective, citing Napster's decentralized peer-to-peer connection as the first 'people web' experience.
- The thing I got wrong with Tribe was trust... for people to put themselves on the web, they first had to feel this good container of trust. Evidence: Pincus's self-reflection on his failed social network, contrasting it with Facebook's success in establishing trust through the .edu domain.
- Neither Reed or I ever imagined how I know we none of us ever imagined how big this Peter Teal all of us we all sold our stock in Facebook so we voted with our shares you know none of us could have ever described how big the this all got. Evidence: Pincus's anecdote about early Facebook investors, including himself, Reed Hoffman, and Peter Thiel, selling their stock, demonstrating that even insiders underestimated its eventual scale.
- AI... is smart enough. [If] you surround it with the right harness and the right context, it can actually do relatively magical things. Evidence: Pincus's current assessment of frontier AI capabilities, based on his personal use and experimentation.
- Siri [is] so bad... And then at Amazon, it's just unbelievable... there's no real like LLM that you could feel. Evidence: Pincus's direct criticism of major tech companies' voice assistants for failing to integrate LLM capabilities effectively, despite large teams.
- AI... is brilliant at proving... The better part I'd give it like a B minus... And then for new I'd give it a D. Evidence: Pincus's assessment of AI's capabilities within the 'Proven Better New' framework, highlighting that AI excels at replication and iteration but struggles with true novelty, which still requires human input.
- 90% of enterprises that have like invested in AI haven't received any benefit yet. Evidence: A statistic cited by the interviewer, suggesting widespread inefficiency and a 'skill issue' in enterprise AI adoption.
- The new way to do it is actually have the LLMs just write the code you need right now... you write 10 or 20 times less code, but it's way more customizable. Evidence: The interviewer's claim about a paradigm shift in AI-assisted coding, moving from writing code that calls LLMs to teaching LLMs to write code directly.
- The consumer revolution is actually in 2029. Evidence: The interviewer's prediction, based on the expectation that AI compute costs will decrease by 'three orders of magnitude' over the next few years, making advanced AI accessible to consumers.
- Anything that can be free will be free. Evidence: Pincus's 'rule of the internet,' supported by examples like Freeloader competing with paid software and Zynga offering free games in a traditionally paid industry.
