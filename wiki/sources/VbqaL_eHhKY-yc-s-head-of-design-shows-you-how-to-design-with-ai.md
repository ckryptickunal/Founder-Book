# YC's Head of Design Shows You How To Design With AI

## Metadata

- Video ID: `VbqaL_eHhKY`
- Channel: Y Combinator
- Published: 2026-07-10T14:00:10Z

## Summary

E Bufar, YC's Head of Design, details her innovative AI-driven design workflow, emphasizing a shift from traditional tools to conversational interfaces and AI agents. She showcases three projects: Paxel, an experiment providing 'Spotify Wrapped'-like feedback on AI coding patterns; Sodazine, a physical zine and interactive website celebrating San Francisco, designed with extensive AI context and rapid iteration; and Startup School, YC's major event, where AI was used for consistent branding, speaker cards, and personalized tickets. The discussion highlights the power of providing comprehensive context to AI, the concept of 'disposable design' for rapid prototyping, and the future of user-driven software development through AI agents.

## Key Ideas

- AI-driven design workflow is becoming the standard, replacing traditional tools and methods for efficiency and creativity.
- Conversational UI (voice control via tools like Aqua) significantly accelerates the design process by allowing designers to 'think faster than they type'.
- The concept of 'disposable design' involves rapidly building custom, temporary tools (e.g., modals for fine-tuning, iteration galleries) with AI to achieve precise design outcomes, then discarding them.
- Providing comprehensive context to AI agents (e.g., through 'soul.md' files, mood boards, example websites) is crucial for generating high-quality, non-generic, and creatively surprising design outputs.
- AI agents can act as creative collaborators, brainstorming and suggesting design elements or features that designers might not have considered, leading to 'AGI moments'.
- The future of software development includes users submitting feature requests directly to AI agents, which can automate pull requests and lead to highly personalized local software experiences.
- AI enables unprecedented speed, consistency, and personalization in branding and visual asset creation, making complex design tasks feasible that were previously 'insurmountable'.
- Analyzing coding transcripts with AI (as in Paxel) provides valuable, fun, and actionable feedback to developers about their patterns and habits, akin to 'Spotify Wrapped' for coding.

## Notable Claims

- E Bufar's workflow is almost exclusively in Conductor and Paper Design, which is all she needs for end-to-end projects. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'I find myself almost exclusively nowadays in conductor and paper design. That's all I need usually to make a full project end to end.'
- E Bufar does not type, preferring to talk to her computer because she thinks faster than she types. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'I do not type. I realize that I think a lot faster than I type... I'd rather talk to my computer instead.'
- A common trend in design is to make graphics 'alive' and provide custom tools for fine-tuning, rather than using static images. Evidence: The interviewer observes: 'This is a a common trend that I've been seeing a lot is rather than generating something having the static images having that, you know, be the the edges of the page and and the graphics on the card, you actually just make it alive and give yourself a custom tool to be able to to turn knobs and dials to get it exactly how you want it.'
- Building custom tools for fine-tuning with AI is a 'muscle' that designers need to develop. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'We realized that it's almost like a muscle that you need to build and train when you realize that you can build anything for yourself whenever you want to fine-tune something.'
- Future websites will likely feature separate versions for humans and for machines/agents. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'I think this is a a pattern that we might start seeing more and more moving forward on websites is there's going to be the the version of the website that is for humans and there's going to be the the version of the website that will be for machines and agents.'
- Agent-driven feature requests, where users submit prompts that fire off agents to open PRs, represent the future of software building. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'I really think that this is the future of where how software will be built in the future.'
- Capturing as much information as possible and sharing it with an AI agent is the best way to build software moving forward. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'Overall, I think capturing as much information as possible and share that information with your agent is the best way to build software moving forward.'
- AI agents can surprise designers with original ideas and brainstorm effectively, sometimes seeing things 'ahead of us', which feels like an 'AGI moment'. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'It's going to include things that you would not have otherwise thought of. And that was almost like an AGI moment for us when we realized that wow it can see things ahead of us and it can really help us brainstorm even and come up with like really really original ideas.'
- AI agents can browse and scrape the web to find and surface cool visuals, animations, and patterns without explicit instruction. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'You're at a point where you don't even know how club does does these things. It just it scrapes the web, it browses the web, it finds cool pictures, animations, and it's going to surface them like this.'
- Building complex design elements like shaders with AI would have been an 'insurmountable mountain' a year ago, but is now magical and easy. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'Could you imagine a year ago trying to build something like this? It wouldn't be worth it. these shaders. Building these shaders a year ago would have been like would have felt like this insurmountable mountain of I would not even have known where to start to build these things. And now it is just this thing that Claude, my claude knows what to pull cuz it it knows that I love paper. It knows that I love their shaders and it's just automatically knows how to pull that all that information from their website and it uses it. It's just really magical.'
- AI-driven design represents a 'different paradigm' for branding design moving forward. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'It is such a different paradigm as to how we even do branding design moving forward.'
- It is easier than ever to achieve design consistency using coding agents for everything. Evidence: E Bufar states: 'It's just easier than ever to make things more consistent and use coding agents for absolutely everything.'
