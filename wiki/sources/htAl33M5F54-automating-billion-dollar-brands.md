## Summary

Confido founders Cara and Justin discuss their journey from a failed fitness startup to building an AI agent workforce platform for major CPG brands like Mars and Unilever. The company automates complex operational, accounting, and sales planning workflows that were previously handled manually or via disparate systems, enabling billion-dollar brands to optimize their P&L and supply chains.

## Key Ideas

- Confido provides an AI agent workforce that automates labor-intensive tasks for consumer packaged goods (CPG) companies.
- The platform replaces manual processes and legacy systems (like Excel or point solutions) with an agentic, cohesive system.
- The founders pivoted from a fitness startup (Outfit) after YC partners advised them to change direction.
- The business model succeeds by positioning itself as a service/labor replacement rather than just software.
- Confido operates without product managers, relying on highly technical engineers who own the entire product lifecycle and customer interaction.
- The platform can reconcile transactions at a granular level (e.g., under $10) that humans previously ignored due to manual constraints.

## Notable Claims

- Confido can reconcile transactions as small as $10, which human teams previously ignored due to manual effort. Evidence: Founders noted that a public company client previously ignored all transactions under $10,000.
- CPG companies are more willing to buy services than traditional software. Evidence: CPG brands are accustomed to paying for third-party brokers and consulting firms to perform labor-intensive tasks.
- The founders closed their first customer using only a Figma prototype before writing code. Evidence: Founders confirmed this during the interview as proof of the visceral pain in the market.
