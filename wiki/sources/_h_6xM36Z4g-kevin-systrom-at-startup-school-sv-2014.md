# Kevin Systrom at Startup School SV 2014

## Summary

Kevin Systrom, co-founder of Instagram, shares his entrepreneurial journey and key lessons learned at Startup School SV 2014. He recounts his early fascination with doing 'lots of different stuff,' leading him to Stanford and his first startup, 'The Tree List.' Systrom emphasizes the importance of learning 'just enough to be dangerous,' embracing a 'bias towards action' over seeking a 'perfect next move,' and how diverse life experiences, like a photography class in Florence, can lay dormant for years before becoming foundational. He details his time at Google and Next Stop, which prepared him for the development of Bourbon, an HTML5 location-sharing app. The pivotal moment came with a suggestion from his girlfriend to add photo filters, leading to the rapid pivot and launch of Instagram. Systrom highlights the critical role of building relationships, being relentless in advocating for your idea, and prioritizing 'Community First' as Instagram's greatest asset.

## Key Ideas

- Failure is tolerated in Silicon Valley, fostering an environment conducive to startups.
- Embrace a 'bias towards action' and prioritize progress over finding a 'perfect next move' in your career.
- Learn 'just enough to be dangerous' to build and launch your ideas; you don't need to be the absolute best at everything.
- Every life experience, no matter how small or seemingly unrelated, can contribute foundational ideas to your startup.
- Surround yourself with great people and go to where the talent is, as relationships are crucial in the startup ecosystem.
- Prioritize user behavior and usage metrics over stated opinions, as actions often speak louder than words.
- Be relentless and your own advocate; most people will tell you your idea won't work, so unwavering belief is essential.
- Community is a startup's greatest asset and is harder to build than technology; prioritize it with a 'Community First' approach.

## Notable Claims

- Failure is tolerated in Silicon Valley, which makes it unique compared to other places. Evidence: Systrom states, 'If you travel overseas to other places there's a reason why Silicon Valley is so different and it's because failure is tolerated.'
- You don't have to be the best at programming, just 'dangerous' enough to build an idea. Evidence: Systrom learned Ruby on Rails 'enough to be dangerous' to build The Tree List, acknowledging that Instagram's 170 employees are much better at it.
- There is no 'perfect next move' in one's career; bias towards action is more important. Evidence: Systrom advises throwing out the idea of a perfect next move and references the Marines' concept of 'bias towards action' for making progress.
- Every little experience, even seemingly unrelated ones, can be foundational for a startup. Evidence: The idea for Instagram's filters came from a photography class in Florence using a Holga camera, an idea that laid dormant for 5 years.
- The best strategy for early career is to 'go to where the people are' and surround yourself with amazing talent. Evidence: Systrom took a lower-paying job at Google because he 'just cared that it was at Google and I would surround myself by amazing people.'
- User actions (usage metrics) are more reliable indicators than what users say they want. Evidence: Regarding The Tree List, Systrom learned that 'what people tell you and how people act are very very different sometimes,' focusing on 'are people using this or not.'
- Founders must be relentless and their own advocates because most people will tell them their idea is bad. Evidence: Systrom states, 'The one thing everyone's going to tell you is that your idea is bad and is never going to work... you have to be your own Advocate.'
- Community is a startup's greatest asset and is harder to build than technology. Evidence: Systrom asserts, 'Anyone can and did build a filter app... But the one thing that's really hard to build is a community.'
