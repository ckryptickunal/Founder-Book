# What Top AI Labs Are Really Doing With Observability

## Summary

Olivier Pomel, co-founder and CEO of Datadog, discusses the company's journey from its inception, overcoming early rejections and challenges, to its current status as a public, multi-product observability leader. He shares insights into Datadog's culture, his leadership philosophy, the impact of the cloud and AI revolutions, and advice on entrepreneurship, emphasizing speed in hiring and firing.

## Key Ideas

- AI is fundamentally changing software development, enabling small teams to achieve what previously required large teams and long timelines, leading to a shift from 'mostly write, sometimes automate' to 'mostly automate, sometimes write'.
- Observability (initially 'monitoring') became critical with the explosion of cloud infrastructure and the need to unify DevSecOps.
- Early rejections (Y Combinator, VCs) fueled motivation and shaped Datadog's unique approach to company building, including a culture that flows from the top rather than being explicitly written.
- Effective leadership involves staying deeply connected to product details and ground-level customer/employee feedback, rather than relying solely on high-level summaries or 'strategic' discussions.
- Public company status shifts focus from fundraising to consistent storytelling, delivering numbers, and managing employee compensation (RSUs) amidst market volatility.
- Entrepreneurs should prioritize speed in hiring and firing, taking calculated risks with hires and quickly course-correcting if they don't fit, rather than seeking a 'perfect' hire or delaying necessary terminations.
- While it's now easier to start a company in Europe, targeting the US market early with a founder on the ground is crucial for scaling.

## Notable Claims

- Excellent developers can rebuild whole systems in a couple of days alone that previously would have taken a team of six over six months, due to AI. Evidence: Observed by Olivier's co-founder and engineering team in recent months.
- Datadog was rejected by Y Combinator twice. Evidence: Olivier received a rejection email from Paul Graham and was later uninvited from a YC dinner.
- The premise of bringing DevSecOps together into one platform was central to the explosion of the cloud. Evidence: Datadog's founding premise aligned perfectly with the unforeseen growth of cloud infrastructure.
- Public investors find it harder to reason about long-term trends like AI compared to VCs. Evidence: Public investors look at many companies and move in/out frequently, unlike VCs who are deeply concentrated and long-term.
- Datadog's co-founder issued a directive that 'in two quarters, we're not writing any code anymore,' signifying a major shift towards automation. Evidence: Statement made by Olivier's co-founder to the engineering team.
- It is absolutely critical for European startups to go after the US market as soon as they have product-market fit. Evidence: Olivier's strong recommendation based on his experience and observations.
