# Open Models Change The Economics of AI

## Summary

Jeffrey Morgan, CEO of Ollama, discusses the shift toward open-source AI models in enterprise, driven by cost efficiency, customization, and the rise of coding agents. He explains Ollama's role as an orchestration layer that simplifies the fragmented AI ecosystem, enabling developers to run models locally or in the cloud while navigating the rapid release cycles of new AI technology.

## Key Ideas

- Open models are increasingly preferred by enterprises for better control and customization, with cost being the initial driver.
- Coding agents and automated workflows are the primary drivers of the recent surge in token consumption.
- The gap between frontier closed models and open models is closing rapidly, making custom fine-tuning more challenging but also more accessible through better tooling.
- Ollama acts as an 'operating system' for AI, abstracting hardware, inference engines, and model harnesses to provide a consistent developer experience.
- A hybrid approach—using local models for straightforward tasks and cloud models for complex reasoning—is becoming the standard enterprise architecture.
- The 'God model' theory is being replaced by a model of orchestration, where smaller, specialized models are chained together to solve complex problems.

## Notable Claims

- Open models will account for 80-90% of token usage within businesses. Evidence: Ollama's internal data on token flow and enterprise adoption trends.
- The gap between closed frontier models and open models is closing to within three months. Evidence: The rapid release cadence of open models like DeepSeek Flash and GLM iterations.
- Local AI inference is becoming viable for 20B-40B parameter models on consumer hardware. Evidence: Performance benchmarks on modern Apple Silicon and Nvidia workstation GPUs.
