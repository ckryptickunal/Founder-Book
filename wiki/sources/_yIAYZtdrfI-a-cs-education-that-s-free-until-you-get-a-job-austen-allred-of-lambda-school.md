# A CS Education That's Free Until You Get a Job - Austen Allred of Lambda School

## Metadata

## Summary

Austen Allred, CEO of Lambda School, discusses the school's innovative income-sharing agreement model for computer science education, which is free until students secure a job. He highlights the challenges of encouraging qualified individuals to apply due to imposter syndrome and addresses concerns about accessibility by offering part-time programs and living stipends. Allred contrasts Lambda School's vocational, outcome-focused approach with traditional universities, emphasizing its 9-month duration, integrated career services, and significant financial advantage for graduates. He also delves into the complexities of scaling a remote education platform, the importance of product quality over business model, and the broader societal issue of mispriced human capital, particularly in underserved communities, which Lambda School aims to address by filling labor market shortages beyond tech.

## Key Ideas

- Lambda School's core model offers free education until employment, addressing financial barriers and risk aversion.
- Overcoming imposter syndrome and encouraging qualified individuals to apply is a significant challenge for Lambda School and similar high-reputation organizations like Y Combinator.
- Traditional higher education (universities) is often misaligned with student success, leading to debt without guaranteed career outcomes, contrasting with Lambda School's vocational focus.
- Lambda School provides a significant competitive advantage over traditional 4-year degrees through faster completion, integrated career services, and network building, leading to earlier career entry and financial gains.
- The business model (Income Share Agreement) is less critical than the quality of the educational product, the effectiveness of the hiring network, and the precision of the admissions process.
- Human capital is an inefficiently priced asset class, with significant discrepancies in opportunity and compensation based on geography and lack of awareness, which Lambda School aims to optimize.
- Future expansion will target other labor market shortages (e.g., nursing) by applying Lambda School's model to address market inefficiencies.
- Effective remote education requires intentional instructional design, data-driven engagement, and a focus on immediate, tangible results rather than abstract fundamentals first.
- Product quality and 'taste' are subtle but crucial differentiators that are often underestimated by quantitative analysis, and can be cultivated by exposing students to well-designed experiences.

## Notable Claims

- The highest leverage activity any human can have is inspiring other humans to do what they're capable of. Evidence: Attributed to Patrick Collison, and Lambda School is presented as a realization of this idea.
- Many Lambda School students suffer from massive imposter syndrome, delaying their job applications despite being fully capable. Evidence: Observation from past students who would wait to apply for jobs, but then quickly get six-figure offers once they started.
- The traditional advice to 'go to college, doesn't matter what it costs or what you study' is fundamentally untrue and often bad advice. Evidence: Observation of student loan debt, people working at Starbucks with degrees, and personal experience (Austen and his brother are not college graduates but are successful).
- By the time a university student graduates, a Lambda School student (who started at the same time) will have 3 years of experience, paid off tuition, and be a quarter-million dollars ahead. Evidence: Comparison of 9-month Lambda School program vs. 4-year university degree, factoring in early career entry and income.
- The best job offers always come through networking and meeting people, not through online 'click and apply' buttons. Evidence: Lambda School's curriculum integrates networking skills, and their best job offers come from informational interviews.
- Human capital is the most unoptimized asset class, lacking an efficient marketplace for pricing and opportunity. Evidence: Comparison to other asset classes traded instantly on Bloomberg, and examples of capable individuals being severely underpaid due to lack of market awareness.
- The 90/10 rule (only 90% of school funds from federal sources) drives massive labor shortages across the US economy, particularly in fields like nursing. Evidence: Explanation of how schools are capped by the difficulty of finding the 10% private funding, leading to unmet demand for skilled workers.
- The market (analysts, VCs) is not very good at measuring product quality, often underestimating its importance. Evidence: Reference to Paul Graham's idea that revenue is a trailing indicator of product, and examples of companies like Airbnb, Stripe, and Tesla winning due to superior product experience despite initial disadvantages.
- Most schools look at successful students and try to weed out others, rather than questioning if their own instructional design is failing the unsuccessful students. Evidence: Austen's thesis that many students are unsuccessful because the school's approach 'sucks,' not because the students 'suck'.
