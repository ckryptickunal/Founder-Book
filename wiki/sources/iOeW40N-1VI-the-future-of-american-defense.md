## Summary

Dan Driskill, the Secretary of the Army, announces a critical shift in the Army's acquisition strategy, urging innovative startups to develop commercially developed, modular open system solutions for modern warfare. He highlights that traditional acquisition methods are too slow for rapidly evolving threats and calls for new technologies such as low-cost interceptors, next-gen sensors, software, cutting-edge drones, resilient logistics, and advanced manufacturing. The Army promises capital and proving grounds to scale these innovations, emphasizing an urgent need for speed and adaptability across all combat environments.

## Key Ideas

- Warfare is at an inflection point, demanding new approaches to defense acquisition.
- The U.S. Army's traditional acquisition methods are too slow to keep pace with modern, rapidly changing threats.
- The Army is actively seeking commercially developed, modular open system solutions from innovative startups.
- Specific technological needs include low-cost interceptors, next-gen sensors, software, payloads, hardware for open architectures, cutting-edge drones, resilient logistics, and advanced manufacturing.
- Solutions must be capable of surviving extreme climates and operating across diverse environments, from Arctic to subterranean.
- The Army is offering capital and proving grounds to help startups scale their defense innovations.
