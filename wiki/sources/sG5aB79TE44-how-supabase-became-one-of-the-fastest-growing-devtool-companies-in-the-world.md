# How Supabase Became One Of The Fastest Growing DevTool Companies In The World

## Metadata

- Video ID: `sG5aB79TE44`

## Summary

Paul Copplestone, co-founder and CEO of Supabase, discusses the company's rapid growth from an open-source Postgres tool in 2020 to a decacorn. He shares his entrepreneurial journey, the strategic choice of Postgres and open source, and the critical role of developer experience. The conversation highlights a significant shift in late 2024 (likely 2023-2024) with the rise of AI agents, which now launch 60-90% of Supabase databases, leading to a 10x growth. Supabase adapted by creating 'Supabase for Platforms' and focusing on infrastructure as code. Copplestone emphasizes that money doesn't solve core problems and outlines Supabase's future bet on 'self-driving databases' to tackle the complex 'operate' stage for AI applications, leveraging their fully remote and well-documented internal processes.

## Key Ideas

- Supabase's rapid growth is attributed to its open-source Postgres foundation and strong developer experience.
- The 'Postgres flywheel' effect, where collective contributions enhance the database due to its open ownership, is a key competitive advantage.
- Prioritizing 'time to value' and listening to the developer community are crucial for dev tool success.
- AI agents have profoundly transformed the dev tools landscape, becoming the primary 'users' for database provisioning and driving exponential growth.
- Supabase is adapting to the AI era by focusing on infrastructure as code, declarative schemas, and branchable database environments.
- Despite significant funding, the core philosophy is that money does not solve fundamental startup problems; competitive advantage comes from other vectors.
- The future of dev tools lies in addressing the 'operate' stage, with Supabase betting on 'self-driving databases' to manage complexity.
- A fully remote, distributed team with comprehensive written documentation provides a unique advantage for AI integration in internal operations.

## Notable Claims

- Supabase is one of the fastest-growing dev tool companies in the world and became a decacorn a few years after founding. Evidence: Stated by the interviewer and confirmed by Paul's discussion of fundraising rounds, including a recent 500M round at over 10B valuation.
- The number one reason startups fail is founders giving up, drama, or not finding product-market fit, rather than solely running out of money. Evidence: Stated by the interviewer based on YC data, and Paul agrees, citing his first startup's failure due to product-market fit and co-founder disagreement.
- Postgres will continue to be the dominant database player over the next 50 years due to a 'flywheel effect' where collective contributions and hyperscaler competition drive continuous improvement. Evidence: Paul states, 'no one owns Postgres, which means that everyone contributes to Postgres. Then all the hyperscalers have to have the best Postgres offering because it's the most popular and therefore Postgres continues to improve.'
- Supabase's initial marketing tagline 'real-time Postgres' did not gain traction, but 'open source Firebase alternative' led to significant growth. Evidence: Paul states, 'I wrote real-time Postgres and it actually didn't really take off and then we switched the tagline to open source Firebase alternative. And then from that point on it really took off.'
- Over 60% of every Y Combinator batch uses Supabase. Evidence: Paul states, 'we get about 60 over 60% of every batch will use Superbase.'
- Cloud providers have historically hosted open-source technologies like Redis or Elastic Search and generated more revenue from them than the original creators. Evidence: Paul states, 'you'll see examples where they might take Redis or Elastic Search or something like this... And then they'll host that and they'll actually make more revenue from that than the companies that created them.'
- Supabase achieves a 'time to value' of approximately 5 seconds, significantly faster than AWS RDS, which remains around 8.5 minutes. Evidence: Paul states, 'these days it's 5 seconds or so' for Supabase, and 'Probably still 8 and 1/2 minutes, yeah' for RDS.
- 60-90% of Supabase databases are now launched by AI agents, with millions launched every month. Evidence: Paul states, 'we can measure 60% of databases get launched by an agent, but more likely it's like 90% and it's in the millions every every month.'
- Supabase experienced approximately 10x growth in users and usage since last year (2025), with all key metrics (users, conversion, activation) accelerating simultaneously. Evidence: Paul states, 'How much like like since last year more like 10x? More than that? Yeah, probably something like that. Which is insane off the base... every chart started going up into the right.'
- Money alone will not solve a startup's fundamental problems and does not guarantee long-term success. Evidence: Paul states, 'the thing that keeps you grounded is just absolutely knowing that money will not solve your problems.' He also contrasts Supabase's success against AWS's greater cash reserves, attributing it to other competitive vectors.
- Supabase's fully remote and distributed model, with all decisions and communications documented in writing, makes it highly compatible with AI agents for internal knowledge retrieval. Evidence: Paul states, 'everything that has ever happened at Superbase has been written down on Slack and Notion... for us to say to an agent, 'Hey, what was that decision that we made 6 years ago?' Uh we can actually prompt it and it'll find it in Slack.'
