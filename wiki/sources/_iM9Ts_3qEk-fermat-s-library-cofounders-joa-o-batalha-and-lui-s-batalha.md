# Fermat's Library Cofounders João Batalha and Luís Batalha

## Metadata

- Video ID: `_iM9Ts_3qEk`

## Summary

João Batalha and Luís Batalha, co-founders of Fermat's Library, discuss their platform for annotating academic papers. They explain its origin from an internal journal club and its mission to foster open science, including open data, code, and publishing, and to enhance scientific collaboration online. The interview covers their arXiv Chrome extension, challenges in scientific publishing like the disincentive to publish negative results and p-value hacking, and the evolution of scientific writing. They also share insights from interesting papers they've read, discuss metrics for scientific impact, and explore the potential for annotating books. The brothers emphasize their passion for the project, its non-profit focus, and the growing trend of open journals and online learning.

## Key Ideas

- Fermat's Library is an online platform for annotating academic papers, supporting LaTeX and Markdown, designed to make complex scientific content more accessible.
- The platform's core mission is to advance the open science movement by promoting open data, open code, open publishing, and remote collaboration among scientists.
- Online collaboration is a powerful tool for scientific discovery, as exemplified by the solution to the Erdos Discrepancy problem and the Polymath Project.
- Fermat's Library developed a Chrome extension for arXiv to address user-requested features like commenting, reference extraction, and improved discoverability for preprints.
- Significant challenges in academic publishing include the lack of incentives for researchers to publish negative results and the prevalence of p-value hacking, both of which can hinder scientific progress.
- Scientific writing has evolved, with older papers often being much shorter and simpler, while modern papers tend to be longer and more formal, raising questions about clarity and accessibility.
- Impactful scientific writing prioritizes clarity and accessibility, and review papers can sometimes have a greater impact than original discovery papers by unifying and disseminating knowledge.
- Traditional metrics for scientific impact, such as citations, are imperfect, and alternative measures like social media engagement or code forks could offer broader insights.
- The current peer review system is flawed and needs significant improvement to ensure rigorous and unbiased evaluation of scientific work.
- The concept of annotating books, especially introductory science texts, holds great potential for collaborative knowledge building and continuous improvement, similar to open-source software.
- Overlay journals, built on top of preprint servers like arXiv, represent a viable model for open-access publishing that can eventually rival the reputation of traditional paywalled journals, especially with endorsement from established scientists.
- There is a significant public appetite for learning scientific concepts, as demonstrated by the success of science communicators on platforms like Twitter, highlighting the addictive nature of acquiring knowledge.
- Fermat's Library operates as a passion project, driven by the founders' enjoyment and commitment to open science, without immediate focus on profitability, relying on community contributions and minimal operational costs.

## Topics

- [[topics/academic-paper-annotation|Academic Paper Annotation]]: The process and benefits of adding explanatory notes, comments, and context to scientific papers, facilitated by platforms like Fermat's Library, to improve understanding and collaboration.
- [[topics/open-science-movement|Open Science Movement]]: The broader initiative to make scientific research, data, code, and publications freely accessible to all, promoting transparency, reproducibility, and faster progress.
- [[topics/scientific-collaboration-online|Scientific Collaboration Online]]: The increasing trend and potential for scientists to collaborate remotely on research problems, leveraging online platforms and tools to share insights and accelerate discoveries.
- [[topics/challenges-in-scientific-publishing|Challenges in Scientific Publishing]]: Discussion of systemic issues within academic publishing, including the disincentive to publish negative results, p-value hacking, and the difficulties of discoverability and quality assessment in a rapidly expanding body of literature.
- [[topics/evolution-of-scientific-writing|Evolution of Scientific Writing]]: An observation on how the length, formality, and accessibility of scientific papers have changed over time, with older papers often being shorter and more direct.
- [[topics/metrics-for-scientific-impact|Metrics for Scientific Impact]]: An exploration of how the value and influence of scientific work are measured, questioning the sole reliance on citation counts and considering alternative indicators like social media engagement or real-world application.
- [[topics/peer-review-system|Peer Review System]]: An analysis of the current system for evaluating scientific manuscripts before publication, acknowledging its imperfections and the need for reform to ensure rigor and fairness.
- [[topics/simpsons-paradox|Simpsons Paradox]]: A statistical phenomenon where a trend appears in different directions when data is grouped versus when it is disaggregated, highlighting the importance of how data is analyzed and presented.
- [[topics/hot-hand-phenomenon|Hot Hand Phenomenon]]: A psychological bias or belief that a person who experiences success with a random event has a greater chance of further success in subsequent attempts, often disproven by statistical analysis.
- [[topics/overlay-journals|Overlay Journals]]: A model of open-access publishing where journals curate and peer-review papers already posted on preprint servers like arXiv, providing a reputable publishing venue without hosting costs or paywalls.
- [[topics/online-learning-and-science-communication|Online Learning and Science Communication]]: The effectiveness of digital platforms, including social media, in disseminating scientific knowledge and fostering a public appetite for learning, often through concise and engaging content.
