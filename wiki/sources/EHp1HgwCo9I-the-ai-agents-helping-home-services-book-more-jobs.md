# The AI Agents Helping Home Services Book More Jobs

## Metadata

## Summary

Avoca, a YC Winter 23 team, has achieved remarkable growth, reaching eight-figure revenue and 2x growth in a 90-day period. Founded by Apurv and Tyson, Avoca is building an AI workforce for the physical economy, starting with home services like HVAC and plumbing. Their core product is an AI agent that answers phone calls, books jobs, and drives revenue for these businesses, addressing the high value of phone calls (85% of revenue) and the poor response rates in the industry. The founders emphasize customer obsession, the transformative potential of AI to capture a larger share of business expenditure (15%+ vs. 1% for traditional software), and how their solution augments human CSRs, improving job satisfaction and retention by handling 'nuisance calls' and creating new roles for training AI agents. They attribute their success to early customer love, strategic growth from referrals, and a strong team, with a vision for Avoca to become a 'system of intelligence' across various industries.

## Key Ideas

- Avoca is building an AI workforce for the physical economy, starting with home services.
- AI agents answer phone calls, book jobs, and generate significant new revenue for home service businesses.
- Home services rely heavily on phone calls for revenue (85%), making AI-driven call handling highly impactful.
- AI solutions can capture a much larger share of business expenditure (15%+ for labor/marketing) compared to traditional software (1%).
- Avoca's AI augments human customer service representatives (CSRs), handling routine calls and allowing humans to focus on higher-value tasks, improving job satisfaction and retention.
- Customer obsession and relentless focus on customer value are critical for startup success, often prioritized over market size by YC.
- Early growth was driven by 'unscalable things' like deep engagement with design partners, referrals, and influencer networks, followed by scaling sales after achieving product-market fit.
- The product is evolving from a singular AI CSR wedge to a broader 'AI workforce' offering proactive outreach, coaching, and targeted marketing.
- The co-founders' prior experiences at companies like Nuro and Retool, and their shared passion for AI, shaped Avoca's development.

## Notable Claims

- Avoca has reached eight digits in revenue and achieved 2x growth in a single 90-day period. Evidence: Stated by the interviewer at the beginning of the podcast and confirmed by the founders.
- 85% of the revenue for a home service business comes from phone calls. Evidence: Stated by Tyson when explaining the high value of home service calls compared to restaurants.
- A single phone call in home services can be worth $20,000 to $30,000. Evidence: Stated by Tyson when comparing the value of calls in home services versus restaurants.
- Traditional software in home services (like ServiceTitan) only captures 1% to 2% of a business's wallet spend. Evidence: Discussed by both the interviewer and Apurv, highlighting the limited scope of traditional software.
- Customer service, inside sales, and marketing combined represent 15%+ of total expenditure for a business. Evidence: Apurv explains that these operational areas offer a much larger 'wallet share' for AI to capture.
- The attrition rate for customer support in a normal home service business is close to 100% over two years. Evidence: Stated by Tyson, describing the high turnover due to the unpleasant nature of the job.
- Avoca's AI agents handle 60-70% of 'nuisance calls' for home service businesses. Evidence: Tyson explains that the AI takes these calls, freeing up human CSRs.
- Avoca's solution leads to improved CSR retention and promotions to new roles like dispatch or AI agent training. Evidence: Tyson provides examples of customers where CSRs were promoted and retention rates skyrocketed.
- Home service businesses frequently switch answering services, sometimes five times in a single year, due to poor quality. Evidence: The founders' experience with their first customer, Rescue Air, whose operations manager Andreas, detailed this problem.
- Avoca achieved early traction through referrals and an 'influencer network' within the home services industry. Evidence: Tyson mentions Josh Campbell from Rescue Air connecting them to many early customers and chatter on Facebook groups.
- Scaling sales before truly finding product-market fit is one of the biggest mistakes that can kill a business. Evidence: Apurv states that they reached over a million in revenue before hiring their first actual sales team.
