# Pick One Idea and Go Deep

## Metadata

- Video ID: `R56RJFZBasQ`

## Summary

John, a partner at Y Combinator, advises startup founders to stop overthinking and searching for the 'perfect' idea. Instead, he advocates for picking a single idea, committing to it fully by 'burning the boats,' and gaining deep domain expertise through direct customer engagement to either validate the business or discover a more viable opportunity.

## Key Ideas

- Avoid overthinking; the perfect idea cannot be found in the abstract, only through real-world feedback.
- Avoid juggling multiple ideas, as it produces poor data and prevents deep understanding.
- Commitment involves 'burning the boats'—foreclosing other options to focus entirely on one path.
- Deep domain expertise is achieved by acting as if you could run the customer's business yourself.
- In the AI era, successful startups should verticalize by owning outcomes rather than just selling software.
- Ambitious ideas are often as difficult to execute as modest ones, so aim for high-impact, sector-rewriting goals.
- Failure after going deep is valuable because it provides concrete data and often reveals the 'real' opportunity hidden beneath surface-level problems.

## Notable Claims

- Juggling multiple startup ideas simultaneously produces bad data. Evidence: Lack of deep focus prevents the collection of high-quality signals regarding whether a product actually solves a customer's problem.
- The cost of pursuing a wildly ambitious startup is roughly the same as a modest one. Evidence: Both paths require extreme effort, time, and dedication from the founders.
- Going deep is the best way to find a better idea underneath the original one. Evidence: Deep engagement reveals structural bottlenecks and gaps that are not visible from the surface.

## Quotes

> You can only figure out what you should be working on by making contact with reality and getting feedback from customers.

> In the AI era, the cost of producing software is going to zero. So, the things that actually become valuable aren't just software for X. They're customer trust, licenses, regulatory permission, and outcome ownership.
