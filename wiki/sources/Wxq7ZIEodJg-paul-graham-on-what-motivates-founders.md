# Paul Graham on what motivates founders

## Summary

Paul Graham explains that while ambition is necessary for founders to endure the grueling nature of startups, the primary day-to-day motivator is not the prospect of wealth, but the immediate fear of failure and the desire to protect their 'model train set'.

## Key Ideas

- Founders require deep ambition to overcome the extreme obstacles inherent in startups.
- Dutifulness is insufficient to sustain a founder through the challenges of building a company.
- Day-to-day motivation is driven by the immediate fear of disaster rather than long-term financial goals.
- Financial success, such as becoming a billionaire, is often a byproduct that surprises founders after years of focused work.

## Notable Claims

- The fear of failure is a more potent day-to-day motivator for founders than the desire to become a billionaire. Evidence: Graham notes that founders focus on immediate crises like server crashes rather than abstract financial milestones.
- Becoming a billionaire often takes successful founders by surprise. Evidence: Graham recounts instances where he has to calculate the valuation for founders who had not realized they reached that status.

## Quotes

> Being dutiful is not enough. The obstacles are too fearsome for mere dutifulness to do it.
> You know what actually motivates founders day-to-day? The fear of failure.
> You put your head down and work on the model train set for 10 years. Then you lift your head up and like, 'Holy... I'm a billionaire.'
