## Summary

Max Hodak, CEO of Science, discusses the critical role of internal infrastructure in scaling deep tech startups. He argues that speed of iteration is the primary determinant of success, which requires building custom software systems to manage purchasing, hiring, and performance reviews rather than relying on generic enterprise tools.

## Key Ideas

- Speed of iteration is the most important factor in startup success.
- Infrastructure (purchasing, hiring, performance management) is the operating system of a company and determines its speed.
- Founders should not delegate their judgment; they must make decisions that make sense to them personally.
- Action produces information; when stuck, founders should take action to break local minima.
- Performance reviews should be continuous and data-driven (e.g., 'Eigen reviews') rather than annual, bureaucratic HR processes.
- Deep tech companies should aim for profitability sooner than commonly advised to avoid the 'money cancer' of constant fundraising.
- Hiring should be treated as a rigorous, data-backed process that avoids bottlenecks by distributing judgment across the company.

## Notable Claims

- Speed of iteration determines startup success. Evidence: The compounding effect of learning faster than competitors makes them irrelevant over time.
- Standard enterprise software (ERP/ATS) creates bottlenecks. Evidence: Commercial tools often force companies to use small groups as gatekeepers, whereas custom software allows for distributed, parallel decision-making.
- BCIs are more of a longevity/healthcare story than an AI story. Evidence: Neural engineering provides large, reliable effect sizes in treating conditions like blindness or Parkinson's compared to traditional pharmacology.
- The best hiring comes from within a company's 'scene' or network. Evidence: Initial hires should come from the community that birthed the company as they already share the language and context.
