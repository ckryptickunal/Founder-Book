# Making Startups Powerful

## Metadata

- Video ID: `pg-making-startups-powerful`
- Channel: Paul Graham (web)
- Published: 2026-09
- URL: https://www.paulgraham.com/powerful.html

## Summary

Paul Graham’s September 2026 office-hours heuristic: ask what would make the company more powerful, not merely what would make more money. Power moves include owning the customer relationship, making money or tokens flow through you, adding network effects (even in unexpected places), going full stack, selling to customers who decide fast, and getting data early. Every such move has to make the product better for the customer or it will not stick.

## Key Ideas

- Asking how to make a startup more powerful often yields step-changes; asking how to make more money usually yields increments.
- Owning the customer relationship and making money flow through you is stronger than remaining a component supplier.
- Network effects can be induced in unexpected products, including by letting users share data or train a model.
- Going full stack — using your technology to do x instead of selling it to companies that do x — can swallow the original customer.
- Selling earlier, to customers who decide fast (including other startups), is a source of power because quality wins on a level field.
- Power strategies only work when the result is better for the customer.

## Notable Claims

- Thinking about power sometimes makes a company orders of magnitude more valuable than thinking about incremental revenue. Evidence: Opening of Making Startups Powerful (September 2026).
- You can only add network effects, take the customer relationship, or go full stack when the result is better for the customer. Evidence: Closing constraint in the essay.
