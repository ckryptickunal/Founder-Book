## Summary

Michael Kratsios, Director of the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, discusses the U.S. government's approach to AI, emphasizing the importance of maintaining American leadership, supporting open-source ecosystems, and creating a unified national regulatory framework to avoid a fragmented 'patchwork' of state laws.

## Key Ideas

- The U.S. government aims to foster innovation by avoiding overly rigid, long-term regulatory 'red lines' that become obsolete as technology evolves.
- There is a strong commitment to supporting both closed and open-source AI models as a strategy for national competitiveness.
- The government's role is to 'shape the arena' for scientific discovery rather than directing the research itself.
- Regulatory policy is highly federated across multiple agencies, requiring the White House to act as a central coordinator.
- The administration prioritizes reducing regulatory barriers for 'born in captivity' technologies like commercial drones and supersonic flight.
- Quantum computing is identified as the next major technological frontier that will receive significant government focus in the coming years.

## Topics

- [[topics/ai-policy|AI Policy]]: Balancing safety and innovation through national standards rather than state-level fragmentation.
- [[topics/open-source|Open Source]]: The administration's commitment to maintaining a vibrant open-source ecosystem to ensure U.S. leadership.
- [[topics/regulatory-philosophy|Regulatory Philosophy]]: Distinction between 'born free' and 'born in captivity' technologies to determine appropriate levels of government oversight.
- [[topics/scientific-discovery|Scientific Discovery]]: The shift from government-led to private-sector-led R&D and the role of AI in accelerating scientific breakthroughs.

## Notable Claims

- The EU AI Act is an example of why setting firm, long-term regulatory thresholds is ineffective. Evidence: The Act was finalized before the invention of modern LLMs like ChatGPT, rendering parts of it obsolete.
- The U.S. government is currently shifting from being the primary funder of R&D to a minority partner. Evidence: Private sector and philanthropy now account for approximately 70% of R&D funding, compared to 30% from the federal government.

## Quotes

> For the American people to have all these benefits that AI is going to offer, we have to make sure that we have a regulatory environment that allows that to succeed.
> You don't want to set very firm red lines in the sand because they ultimately don't work.
> The government's job is to shape the arena, not direct discovery.
> There is no place where you can work on bigger problems than the US government.
