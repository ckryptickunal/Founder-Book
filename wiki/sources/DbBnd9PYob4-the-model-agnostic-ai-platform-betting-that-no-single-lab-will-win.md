# The Model-Agnostic AI Platform Betting That No Single Lab Will Win

## Metadata

- Video ID: `DbBnd9PYob4`
- Channel: Y Combinator
- Published: 2026-07-23T14:00:16Z

## Summary

Stan Hulu, co-founder of Dust, discusses building a model-agnostic AI platform for the workplace amidst the dominance of frontier labs like OpenAI and Anthropic. He shares insights from his time at OpenAI, Dust's strategic decision to focus on a horizontal platform, and the challenges of fundraising and maintaining margins in a rapidly evolving AI landscape. Hulu emphasizes the importance of a clear vision for entrepreneurs and the need for verticalized AI products to build defensible network effects.

## Key Ideas

- The future of work will be profoundly transformed by AI, making current work paradigms seem obsolete.
- AI capabilities are not plateauing, leading to continuous disruption and innovation in the industry.
- Horizontal, model-agnostic AI platforms offer a crucial advantage over labs, which are inherently tied to their own models, providing flexibility and choice.
- Frontier labs significantly impact the funding market by absorbing large amounts of capital, making it challenging for smaller startups.
- Sustainable company building involves raising at reasonable valuations and prioritizing product-market fit (PMF) over excessive early funding to avoid a 'coffin corner'.
- Verticalized AI products need strong network effects to build a defensible moat against powerful labs, as intelligence itself becomes commoditized.
- Credit-based pricing is essential for AI product layers to maintain margins amidst exploding usage and increasing token consumption.
- Open-source AI models are crucial for recalibrating the market and pressuring the high margins (estimated 70-80%) of frontier labs.
- A strong, driving vision is paramount for entrepreneurs to sustain through the daily challenges and 'shitty' aspects of company building.

## Notable Claims

- The labs would not be able to be model-agnostic. Evidence: Their business model is inherently tied to their own energy/tokens, similar to an energy provider whose plug only works with their own energy.
- Work would be incredibly disrupted by AI technology. Evidence: The way developers work has 'completely changed' since November 2025, and similar changes are starting to be seen in other functions.
- The AI capability plateau is not happening at all. Evidence: Work is continuously being redefined, indicating ongoing rapid innovation in AI.
- A lot of the energy in the funding market is being absorbed by the labs. Evidence: VCs prefer to invest 1 billion in one lab (e.g., Anthropic) for quick returns rather than distributing smaller amounts across many startups.
- There is a true 'coffin corner' for companies if they raise too much and do not realize the potential of the money raised. Evidence: Observation of friends getting 'burned' in 2020-2021, leading to inevitable downturns.
- Intelligence is commoditized in a way. Evidence: As models get better, the value of 'scaffolding' built around them diminishes, shifting the focus to other forms of defensibility.
- If you have a flat price for AI products, the margins get compressed and will keep compressing. Evidence: Exploding usage, longer agent loops, and models using more tokens lead to increased costs for providers with flat pricing.
- Frontier AI labs are likely creating margins of something like 70 or 80% when serving their models. Evidence: Comparison of latency (as a proxy for cost) and pricing between frontier models (e.g., Claude Code) and equivalent open-source models (e.g., GLM 5.2) shows a roughly 9x price difference.
